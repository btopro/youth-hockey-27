This is a game first. It's also a way to show why the ADM recommendations matter, and to poke at the darker side of youth sports: money, influence and exposure, as opposed to kids just having a great time.

The stands
----------

Every kid on the ice has their own section of fans, from one to eight. When a kid does something good, their super fans go wild, jumping far higher than anyone around them. When a kid takes a penalty or a big hit, their parents might start a fight with the other kid's parents.

Exposure
--------

Every player has a rating called exposure: money and pressure from the stands. It starts with how invested the parents are, and it goes up with showcase packages, hallway interviews and envelopes passed to the coach. In the game, the kids with the most exposure pass the least. Watch for the dollar bills: parents of high-exposure kids wave them when their kid has the puck, so the kid keeps it.

The other rating is coachability: how well a player keeps their position, stays onside and moves the puck. Low coachability chases the puck, and takes more stick penalties.

Intermissions
-------------

*   A parent offers you money for more ice time. Take it, and their kid double-shifts, wears out and plays worse, and the crowd turns on you.
*   A player wants to thank their grandparents for the new bike they got for a hat trick last game. Do they keep talking about the bike, or about their teammates?
*   A man in a quarter-zip is selling a $4,800 showcase package.
*   Two sets of parents are arguing about a missed offsides call while the kids watch from the bench.
*   The grandparents brought orange slices for everyone.

The phone replay
----------------

After some goals, you'll see the replay the way a parent saw it: shaky, on their phone, while they call their kid's agent to argue for spending more money on exposure.

Look closely at the rosters
---------------------------

Every player keeps the same name and number at every age level. Play the same team at 8U and then at 18U, and see who the best players turn out to be.
